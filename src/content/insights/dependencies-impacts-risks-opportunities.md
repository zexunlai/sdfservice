Nature-related discussions often move quickly between dependencies, impacts, risks and opportunities. Keeping the concepts distinct makes analysis clearer.

Dependencies describe the ecosystem services on which an activity relies. Impacts describe how an activity changes nature. Risks concern the potential consequences of dependencies, impacts and wider environmental change. Opportunities concern actions that can reduce harm, improve resilience or create beneficial outcomes.

## Follow the causal chain

A practical analysis can begin with activities and locations, then examine interactions with nature. This creates a traceable chain from what an organization does to the environmental context, the dependencies and impacts involved, and the possible consequences.

The categories interact. A dependency on reliable water supply may create risk when watershed condition declines. An impact on habitat may contribute to regulatory, operational or reputational consequences. An intervention can address more than one part of the chain.

The value of the framework lies in disciplined questions rather than labels. Each claim still needs evidence, a suitable spatial and temporal scale, and a clear account of uncertainty.
