Measurement, reporting and verification—often shortened to MRV—describe connected but distinct functions in an environmental claim.

Measurement concerns how relevant variables are observed or estimated. Reporting organizes the methods, data and calculations into a traceable record. Verification provides independent assessment against defined requirements.

## Design from the claim backward

A monitoring system should begin with the outcome being claimed and work backward to the variables, frequency, instruments, sampling and quality controls required. Collecting more data is not automatically better if the data do not resolve the key uncertainty.

Transparency matters alongside precision. Assumptions, missing data, model choices and uncertainty procedures affect interpretation. A credible report allows another qualified reader to understand the calculation and its limitations.

Digital tools, sensors and remote sensing can strengthen MRV, but technology does not replace methodological fit. Evidence must match the process, geography, scale and time period of the claim.
