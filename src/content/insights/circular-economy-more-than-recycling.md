Recycling is visible and familiar, so it often becomes shorthand for the circular economy. Yet recycling acts late in a product or material life cycle. Circularity begins earlier, with decisions about design, materials, business models, use and recovery.

A circular strategy asks how products and materials can remain useful at their highest value for longer. Durability, maintenance, repair, reuse, refurbishment and remanufacturing can preserve more embedded value than breaking products down into raw materials.

## Think in systems

An intervention that improves one stage may shift burdens elsewhere. A reusable product may require different logistics. A lightweight material may be difficult to recover. A recycling target may increase collection without creating demand for recovered material.

System boundaries therefore matter. Analysis should identify which life-cycle stages, actors and material flows are included, and which outcomes are being optimized. Environmental effects also need evaluation rather than assumption.

Recycling remains important, but it is one strategy within a wider hierarchy. A useful circular economy conversation begins with the function people need and asks how the whole system can provide it with less resource extraction and waste.
