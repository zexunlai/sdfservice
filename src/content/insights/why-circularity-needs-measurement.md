Circular economy strategies are easy to describe in positive terms. Measurement tests whether an intervention changes material use, product life, waste or environmental outcomes in the way intended.

No single circularity metric answers every question. Material input indicators, product-life measures, recovery rates and value-retention measures describe different parts of a system. Their usefulness depends on the decision and the boundary being studied.

## Activity is not outcome

Counting repair programs or recycled content can show activity. It does not by itself demonstrate lower emissions, reduced extraction or better ecosystem outcomes. Environmental assessment may need additional indicators and life-cycle evidence.

Measurement also exposes trade-offs. Increasing recycled input may affect durability; extending product life may require more maintenance; recovery can consume energy. These observations do not invalidate circular strategies. They help improve them.

A credible measurement approach defines its purpose, unit, boundary, data sources and limitations. This creates a foundation for comparison and learning rather than a score detached from context.
