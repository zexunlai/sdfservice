Climate disclosure can improve transparency, but reporting is not the final objective. Its practical value comes from helping an organisation understand how climate-related conditions may affect decisions, strategy and resilience.

## Begin with the decision

A useful process starts by identifying the decisions that climate information should inform. These may concern assets, operations, capital allocation, transition planning or governance. The question shapes the data, time horizon and level of detail required.

Scenario analysis can help examine different possible futures without treating any scenario as a forecast. Emissions information can show where change is most material. Spatial evidence can clarify where physical hazards and exposed activities coincide.

## Keep the evidence traceable

Clear definitions, boundaries and assumptions allow readers to understand what an indicator represents. Uncertainty should be communicated directly rather than hidden behind a single score.

Disclosure supports decision-making when it creates a repeatable connection between evidence, accountability and action. The strongest reporting process is one that helps people ask better questions before a reporting deadline arrives.
